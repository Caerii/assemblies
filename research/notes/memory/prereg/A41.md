restored the safe rule at large areas, but under it the critical load still

### Interpretation, stated now

* P1 to P4 pass: the compiler's recovery time is n/k for small areas, where
  it buys up to ~ 1.6 times the budget, and n/k / 2 for large ones; the
  crossover is read from the gains.
* P1 fails: the peak was particular to the probed cells.
* P2 or P4 fails: the peak persists at large areas at these k p, and the
  n/k = 300 probe cell (k p / ln n = 3.7, p = 0.7) was the exception.
* P3 fails: tau = n/k raises the cliff but not its 90% point everywhere.
* A failed bar is recorded as failed and not moved.

The run is UNJUDGED until P1 to P4 are evaluated and recorded below.

**Reading.** A recovery time equal to the steps between a neuron's uses buys
65% more budget at n/k = 20 and 24% at n/k = 50, nothing at 100, and costs 9%
at 200. Under Amendment 39's rule the critical load is nearly constant --
rho_50 = 0.122, 0.123, 0.122 at n/k = 20, 50, 100 and 0.112 at 200 -- so
the scatter of the earlier cells was mostly tau sitting at different places
on this curve. The compiler's recovery time: tau = n/k up to n/k ~ 50,
tau = n/k / 2 from n/k ~ 100; the safe rule holds under both (rho_90 >=
0.093), narrowly with tau = n/k at n/k = 200.

**Seen in the record, not tested.** Where tau = n/k is the worse arm (n/k =
100 and 200), its brains fail at step 91 to 192 -- about n/k, the first time
replay reaches states written when the neurons of the first states were
reused -- rather than early, as every other failure in Amendments 37 to 41.
That is the tiling deadline of Amendment 28 returning: the peak's right side
is the never-recovering refraction's failure, reached at large areas as soon
as tau reaches n/k.
