\begin{abstract}
We set up the assembly-calculus substrate as a statistical-mechanical system:
microstates (the connectome and the firing pattern), macrostates (overlap,
retrieval accuracy, margin), and control parameters (load $\alpha$, gain $g$,
afferent number $\mu$, depth $D$). Within this frame, a collapse previously
described as a ``depth ceiling'' is identified as a transition in a single
control parameter, the \emph{gain} $g=(1+\beta)^T$. The boundary $g_c$ is
invariant to how $\beta$ and $T$ factorise the gain and invariant to connection
density $p$, but it is \emph{not} a constant of the substrate: it grows with
system size and falls with load, measured as $g_c = 1.715,\ 1.915,\ 2.176$ for
$n = 2000,\ 4000,\ 8000$ at fixed load. A constant $g_c$ is rejected by two
orders of magnitude in residual. The transition width, however, does not shrink
over the same range, so we describe this as a \emph{sharp crossover with a
size-dependent location} rather than a critical point, and say explicitly where
that reading is limited by grid resolution. Statements below are marked
\PROVED, \MEASURED\ or \OPEN; nothing is asserted as proved that has only been
observed, and one registered numerical prediction is recorded together with its
failure.

Section~\ref{sec:budget} adds the sequence memory's budget, the cost model an
assembly compiler needs: a derived interference load $\rho = Lk\ln n/(n^2p)$
with a measured critical value; a recovery time matched to the interval
between a neuron's uses; failure as a large deviation, whose cliff moves with
the logarithm of what is stored; recurring words coded as tokens that carry a
type trace (an intraclass correlation of $0.1$); and a link budget between
areas set by the source area alone. A conjecture that every knob acts through
the dispersion of interference was measured before registration and fails in
its strong form; it is recorded with its numbers.
\end{abstract}

\section{The substrate}

\begin{definition}[Area and assembly]
An \emph{area} $A$ consists of $n$ neurons with a firing cap $k \ll n$. A
\emph{state} of $A$ is a set $x \subseteq [n]$ with $|x| = k$. There are
$\binom{n}{k}$ such states; we call this the \emph{configuration space} of $A$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Connectome and microstate]
A \emph{fiber} from area $A$ to area $B$ carries weights $W \in
\mathbb{R}^{n_B \times n_A}$. At initialisation $W_{ij} \sim
\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$ independently. The \emph{microstate} of the system is
the pair $(\{W^{(f)}\}_f, \{x_A\}_A)$: every synaptic weight together with the
current firing set of every area.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dynamics]
In one round, neuron $i \in B$ receives
\(
  I_i = \sum_{A \to B} \sum_{j \in x_A} W^{(A\to B)}_{ij},
\)
and the new state is $x_B' = \wta(I)$, the $k$ largest coordinates (ties broken
by index). Plasticity is multiplicative and Hebbian: for $j \in x_A$ and $i \in
x_B'$,
\[
  W_{ij} \;\longleftarrow\; \min\!\big(W_{ij}\,(1+\beta),\; w_{\max}\big).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Gain]\label{def:gain}
If a source assembly is presented for $T$ consecutive rounds and the target
assembly is stable across them, every fiber from source to target is
potentiated $T$ times. The resulting multiplicative advantage
\[
  \boxed{\,g \;=\; (1+\beta)^T\,}
\]
is the \emph{gain}. It is the natural coupling constant of the system: $\beta$
and $T$ enter the dynamics only through this product over a stable
presentation.
\end{definition}

\section{Macrostates: order parameters}

\begin{definition}[Overlap]
For assemblies $a,b$ in the same area, $q_{ab} = |a \cap b| / k$. For a family
$\{a_m\}_{m=1}^{M}$ the \emph{spread} is the mean pairwise overlap
$\bar q = \binom{M}{2}^{-1}\sum_{m<m'} q_{a_m a_{m'}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Overlap floor]\label{lem:floor} \PROVED
If $a,b$ are independent uniformly random $k$-subsets of $[n]$ then
$\E[q_{ab}] = k/n$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$|a \cap b| = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{1}[i \in a]\mathbf{1}[i \in b]$. The two
indicators are independent with means $k/n$, so $\E|a\cap b| = n(k/n)^2 =
k^2/n$, and dividing by $k$ gives $k/n$.
\end{proof}

The floor $q_0 = k/n$ is size dependent, so raw overlap cannot be compared
across system sizes. We therefore use the normalised order parameter
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Q}
  Q \;=\; \frac{\bar q - q_0}{1 - q_0} \in [0,1],
\end{equation}
which vanishes for mutually independent assemblies at any $n$ and equals $1$
under total collapse. $Q$ plays the role of the Edwards--Anderson overlap in
spin glasses, and of the retrieval overlap in the Hopfield model.

Two further macrostates are measured. \emph{Retrieval accuracy} $R$ is the
probability that a re-presented item's read-out has its largest overlap with
the correct stored assembly. \emph{Margin} $r$ is the ratio of best to
second-best overlap; it is the more sensitive statistic, moving well before $R$
departs from $1$.

\section{Control parameters}

\begin{align}
  \alpha &= \frac{Mk}{n} && \text{\emph{load}: fraction of the substrate committed}\\
  g &= (1+\beta)^T && \text{\emph{gain}: Definition~\ref{def:gain}}\\
  \mu &= kp && \text{\emph{afferent number}: mean inputs from one assembly}\\
  D && &\text{\emph{depth}: levels of composition}
\end{align}

The load $\alpha$ is the exact analogue of the Hopfield storage ratio. The
afferent number $\mu$ has no Hopfield counterpart because that model is fully
connected; here it is decisive, and its role is the subject of
Proposition~\ref{prop:mu}.

\section{Measured phase structure}

\emph{Historical evidence: the measurements of this section and the next two
are on two seeds, before the twenty-brain rule; not cited by any manuscript
until rerun.}

At $\alpha = 0.8$, $\mu = 2.5$, $D = 5$, three regimes are distinguished by the
\emph{pair} $(Q, R)$ --- neither alone separates them.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Phase & $Q$ & $R$ & Mechanism \\
\midrule
Starved   & $\approx 0$ & $<1$ & assemblies distinct but drive too weak to retrieve\\
Retrieval & $\approx 0$ & $\approx 1$ & the working phase \\
Collapsed & $\to 1$ & $\to 0$ & assemblies merge on contact \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

That the starved and collapsed phases are \emph{both} low-$R$ while differing
in $Q$ is the practically important fact: they require opposite corrections
(raise $g$ versus lower $g$), and a diagnostic keyed on $R$ alone cannot tell
them apart.

\begin{proposition}[Critical gain]\label{conj:gc} \MEASURED\ (two seeds; not registered)
For $T \ge 2$ and at fixed $(n,\alpha,\mu,D)$ there is a critical gain $g_c$
such that composition to depth $5$ succeeds for $g < g_c$ and fails for
$g > g_c$, and $g_c$ depends on $\beta$ and $T$ only through $g$. At $n=4000$,
$\alpha=0.8$, $\mu=2.5$, $D=5$ the boundary lies at $g_c \approx 1.9$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{$g_c$ is not a constant of the substrate.} The size qualification
above is not pedantry. A fine scan at matched load gives, by the $R=1/2$
crossing, $g_c \approx 1.71$ at $n=2000$ against $\approx 1.9$ at $n=4000$: the
critical gain \emph{rises with system size}. This is consistent in direction
with the separately measured threshold for recurrence persistence, which also
rises with $n$. Any statement of the form ``$g_c \approx 2$'' is therefore a
statement about one system size, and the object of interest is the function
$g_c(n,\alpha,\mu,D)$ --- which is what Conjecture~\ref{conj:fss} is really
about, and what makes the $\sqrt{2\ln(n/k)}$ term in
Conjecture~\ref{conj:why} the right shape to be looking for.

Evidence ($n=4000$, $\alpha=0.8$, $\mu=2.5$, $D=5$, 2 seeds): $\beta$ ranges
over $0.18$--$0.45$ across the boundary while $g$ does not move.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
\toprule
$T$ & $\beta$ & $g=(1+\beta)^T$ & $R$ & $\bar q$ \\
\midrule
3 & 0.18 & 1.643 & 1.000 & 0.0157 \\
2 & 0.30 & 1.690 & 1.000 & 0.0180 \\
3 & 0.22 & 1.816 & 0.883 & 0.0254 \\
2 & 0.35 & 1.823 & 0.938 & 0.0247 \\
2 & 0.40 & 1.960 & 0.164 & 0.0812 \\
3 & 0.26 & 2.000 & 0.117 & 0.2359 \\
2 & 0.45 & 2.103 & 0.070 & 0.2691 \\
3 & 0.32 & 2.300 & 0.047 & 0.6170 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

$T=1$ lies systematically below this curve (at $g=1.800$ it gives $R=0.625$
where $T\in\{2,3\}$ give $0.938$ and $0.883$), so the collapse onto $g$ is
asserted only for $T \ge 2$. A single round does not let the target assembly
converge before plasticity is applied, which is a separate effect and is not
described by $g$.

\begin{proposition}[Density sets the level, not the location]\label{prop:mu} \MEASURED
Doubling $p$ from $0.05$ to $0.10$ (i.e.\ $\mu$ from $2.5$ to $5$) raises the
depth-5 margin from $4.65$ to $8.11$ and $R$ from $0.938$ to $1.000$ at
$\beta=0.35$, while leaving the failure at $\beta=0.40$ unchanged
($R = 0.164 \to 0.172$). Moreover the \emph{sign} of $\partial r/\partial
\beta$ depends on $\mu$: for $\mu \lesssim 1$ the margin has an interior
maximum in $\beta$, and for $\mu \gtrsim 2.5$ it is increasing over the tested
range.
\end{proposition}

Interpretation: with few afferents, potentiation concentrates drive on a
handful of neurons that then win every subsequent competition, so large $\beta$
manufactures collapse; with many afferents the same potentiation is spread
across a rich pattern and selectivity survives. Density therefore controls the
\emph{amplitude} of the signal but not the \emph{stability boundary}.

\section{What remains to be derived}

\subsection{An empirical law for \texorpdfstring{$g_c$}{g\_c}}

Untangling $n$ from $M$ --- the main scan holds $\alpha$ fixed and so moves
both at once --- gives three cells that separate the two variables:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccccl}
\toprule
$n$ & $M$ & $\alpha$ & $g_c$ & \\
\midrule
2000 & 32 & 0.8 & 1.715 & \\
4000 & 32 & 0.4 & 2.260 & $n$ doubled at fixed $M$: $\Delta = +0.545$\\
4000 & 64 & 0.8 & 1.917 & $M$ doubled at fixed $n$: $\Delta = -0.343$\\
2000 & 64 & 1.6 & $<1.50$ & below the scanned range\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both variables move $g_c$, and in opposite directions, so neither the
fresh-pool picture ($n$ only) nor the stored-set picture ($M$ only) is
complete on its own. Reading the two increments as logarithmic gives
$\partial g_c/\partial \ln n \approx 0.79$ and
$\partial g_c/\partial \ln M \approx -0.50$, and substituting $M = \alpha n/k$
collapses these to

\begin{equation}\label{eq:gclaw}
  g_c \;\approx\; C \;+\; 0.29\,\ln n \;-\; 0.50\,\ln \alpha .
\end{equation}

\begin{conjecture}[Empirical form of $g_c$]\label{conj:gclaw} \OPEN\ (a three-point fit; its registered prediction missed by $20\%$ of the effect, below)
$g_c$ increases logarithmically with system size at fixed load and decreases
logarithmically with load, as in \eqref{eq:gclaw}.
\end{conjecture}

Three points determine three coefficients, so \eqref{eq:gclaw} is not yet
tested --- it is a fit. Its content is the \emph{prediction} it makes at a
fourth cell. For $n=8000$, $M=128$ (again $\alpha=0.8$, both doubling once
more) it requires
\[
  g_c(8000,128) \;\approx\; 1.917 + 0.29\ln 2 \;\approx\; \boxed{2.12}.
\]
This was registered before that cell finished measuring.

\paragraph{Outcome: the prediction missed.} The measured value is
$g_c(8000,128) = 2.176$, against the predicted $2.12$ --- an error of $0.056$
on an increment of $0.259$, so roughly $20\%$ of the effect. The qualitative
content survived and the quantitative coefficient did not: the increments at
$\alpha=0.8$ are $+0.202$ then $+0.259$, i.e.\ \emph{growing}, whereas a
logarithm in $n$ requires equal increments per doubling. Refitting on all three
sizes gives $\partial g_c/\partial\ln n \approx 0.333$ rather than the $0.291$
inferred from the two-cell decomposition.

What is nonetheless settled is the qualitative question. Against the three
sizes at $\alpha = 0.8$ ($g_c = 1.715,\ 1.915,\ 2.176$):
\[
  \text{constant: } \mathrm{resid}\ 0.107, \qquad
  \log: 0.0006, \qquad
  \sqrt{2\ln(n/k)}: 0.0010 .
\]
A constant $g_c$ is decisively rejected --- two orders of magnitude worse ---
so \textbf{$g_c$ has no thermodynamic limit within the tested range}. The
logarithmic and extreme-value forms are not distinguishable from one another at
three sizes, and no claim is made between them.

\paragraph{It is a crossover, not a critical point.} The logistic fits also
return a transition \emph{width}. At $\alpha=0.8$ these are $0.035$, $0.055$
and $0.040$ for $n = 2000, 4000, 8000$: the boundary \emph{moves} with size but
does not \emph{sharpen}. A continuous critical transition requires
$w \sim n^{-1/\nu} \to 0$. Consistently, the collapse scan rails at the largest
$\nu$ offered ($\nu = 6$, the edge of the search), which is the scan's way of
saying that subtracting $g_c(n)$ alone already aligns the curves and no
rescaling of the axis is wanted.

We therefore \textbf{withdraw the term ``critical point''} in favour of
\emph{sharp crossover with a size-dependent location}, and
Conjecture~\ref{conj:fss} is correspondingly disfavoured rather than supported.

One caveat keeps this from being final: the gain grid has spacing $0.04$--$0.06$
near the boundary, so widths of $0.035$--$0.055$ sit at the resolution limit.
The honest statement is that the width is \emph{not resolved to shrink}, not
that it provably does not. Settling it requires a grid refined around each
$g_c(n)$ separately, which is the next measurement.

The sign structure is worth stating plainly, because it is the practical
content: \textbf{bigger substrates tolerate more gain, and heavier loads
tolerate less.} A gain that is safe on a small vocabulary can therefore become
unsafe purely by adding items --- with no change to any plasticity parameter.

\begin{conjecture}[Finite-size scaling]\label{conj:fss} \OPEN
Near $g_c$ the order parameter obeys
\[
  Q(g,n) \;=\; n^{-\gamma/\nu}\, F\!\big((g-g_c)\, n^{1/\nu}\big)
\]
for a scaling function $F$ and exponents $\gamma,\nu$; equivalently, curves of
$Q$ against $g$ at different $n$ cross at $g_c$ and collapse when replotted
against $(g-g_c)n^{1/\nu}$.
\end{conjecture}

This is the statement that distinguishes a genuine continuous transition from a
mere threshold, and it is what \texttt{critical\_point\_scan.py} is measuring.
A threshold set by a fixed arithmetic condition would show no $n$ dependence at
all; a first-order transition would show hysteresis instead of collapse.

\begin{conjecture}[Why $g_c \approx 2$]\label{conj:why} \OPEN
The observed $g_c$ lies close to $2$, the point at which one stable
presentation doubles a fiber's weight. A derivation should compare the
potentiated drive of a stored assembly's members against the upper order
statistics of the fresh candidate pool: with afferent counts
$\mathrm{Binomial}(k,p)$, the $k$-th largest of $n$ candidates sits
approximately $\sqrt{2\ln(n/k)}$ standard deviations above the mean, giving a
condition of the form $g > 1 + c\sqrt{(1-p)/(kp)}\,\sqrt{2\ln(n/k)}$ for a
veteran to displace fresh candidates.
\end{conjecture}

We flag a tension that must be resolved rather than glossed: the right-hand
side of that expression depends on $p$, whereas $g_c$ was measured to be
\emph{independent} of $p$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:mu}). Either the relevant
comparison is not against fresh candidates but against \emph{other stored}
assemblies --- whose drive is potentiated by the same $g$, cancelling the
density dependence --- or the argument is wrong. The first alternative is the
more likely and is the natural next derivation.

\begin{proposition}[Depth is information-limited]\label{prop:dpi} \PROVED\ (the bound; its value is unmeasured)
Model a composition chain as a channel $X \to A_1 \to \cdots \to A_D$ where
$X$ is the item identity, uniform on $M$ values. By the data-processing
inequality $I(X; A_1) \ge \cdots \ge I(X; A_D)$, and exact retrieval at depth
$D$ requires $I(X;A_D) = \log M$. Hence if each level loses at least $\delta$
bits, the maximum reliably composable depth is at most
$(\log M - \log M_{\min})/\delta$.
\end{proposition}

This turns depth decay into a measurable quantity --- $\delta$ is estimable
from the confusion matrix at each level --- and gives a bound that does not
depend on the details of $\wta$. Making it quantitative requires estimating
$I(X;A_d)$ at each depth $d$ from the empirical read-out distribution, which the scan's per-level
records already support.

\section{Operating point: is the substrate quasicritical?}

A measured crossover (the discussion before Conjecture~\ref{conj:fss} withdraws the term ``critical point'') invites the question the critical-brain literature
asks of cortex: does the system work best \emph{at} criticality, or near it?
Here the question is sharp because $g$ is an explicit parameter rather than
something the system must self-organise towards.

\paragraph{A scope caveat, stated plainly.} This substrate is not a spiking
network. It has no membrane dynamics, no continuous time, and no avalanches;
one ``round'' is a synchronous $k$-WTA update. So the classical signatures of
critical brain dynamics --- avalanche size and duration distributions with
exponents near $-3/2$ and $-2$, and the associated crackling-noise relation ---
are \emph{not} measurable here, and any claim of continuity with those results
would be an equivocation. What transfers is the weaker and still useful
statement: a control parameter with a critical value, an order parameter that
changes character across it, and an operating point defined relative to it.

\begin{proposition}[The optimum lies strictly below $g_c$]\label{prop:sub} \MEASURED
At depth $5$, margin is not maximised as $g \to g_c^-$. At $T=2$, $g=1.690$
gives margin $6.53$ while $g=1.823$ gives $4.65$; at $T=3$, $g=1.643$ gives
$9.65$ while $g=1.816$ gives $4.85$. Performance degrades on approach to the
transition from below, before the transition itself.
\end{proposition}

This is the opposite of ``tune to the critical point''. It suggests the useful
regime is \emph{subcritical with headroom}, and it has a natural reading: the
approach to $g_c$ is where overlap begins to accumulate, and at depth that
accumulation compounds multiplicatively along the chain.

\begin{conjecture}[Depth lowers the optimal gain]\label{conj:depth-gain} \OPEN
The gain maximising margin decreases with composition depth $D$. At $D=3$ the
margin is still increasing at $\beta=0.35$ ($4.70 \to 7.89 \to 8.97$ for
$\beta = 0.10, 0.20, 0.35$); at $D=5$ an interior maximum appears near
$\beta \approx 0.30$.
\end{conjecture}

The evidence for Conjecture~\ref{conj:depth-gain} is currently assembled across
runs with differing seeds and is the weakest claim in this document; it is
stated because it is directly testable on the scan data, not because it is
established.

\paragraph{Consequence: a per-area gain schedule.} If the optimal gain falls
with depth, then a single global $\beta$ is provably suboptimal for a
hierarchy, and areas deeper in the composition chain should carry lower gain.
Since $g=(1+\beta)^T$, this can be realised either by lowering $\beta$ per area
or by shortening $T$ per level --- the two are interchangeable to the extent
Conjecture~\ref{conj:gc} holds, which is itself a testable prediction about a
design choice. A time-varying schedule (high gain early to recruit, decaying
later to protect what is stored) is the same statement along the training axis
rather than the spatial one, and is the natural analogue of a
neuromodulatory gating of plasticity.


\section{The memory's phase diagram in the write--load plane}

The sections above concern composition depth. The refracted assembly memory
(\texttt{PREREG\_refraction\_memory.md}, Amendments 9--23) has a second phase
structure, along the write strength $\beta$ and the load $M$ (items stored),
measured with two order parameters at once: \emph{recall} $R_c$, the fraction
of half-cued items whose read-out recovers at least $0.8$ of the item and is
nearer it than any other stored item, and \emph{recognition} $R_r$, the
fraction whose read-out is merely nearer the cued item than any other (rank-1).
$R_r$ has a chance floor of $1/M$; it is read only from $M \ge 32$.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../experiments/figures/memory_phase_diagram.png}
\caption{Load windows (where each order parameter exceeds $1/2$) against
$\beta/\theta$, $\theta = \sqrt{(1-p)\ln n/(pk)}$, at $p = 1/2$, twenty brains per
cell (Amendment 23 record; \texttt{memory\_phase\_diagram.py}). Blue:
recognition; red: recall; dashed: the two onsets.}
\end{figure}

\begin{proposition}[Four regions, two onsets]\label{prop:phases} \MEASURED
In every one of seven cells ($n = 2000$--$8000$, $k = 10$--$160$) the plane
divides into: \emph{no memory} below a recognition onset $\beta_r$ (rank-1 at
chance at every load); \emph{recognition only}, between $\beta_r$ and the
recall onset $\beta_c$, and at loads past recall's window; \emph{recall}, inside
the recall window above $\beta_c$; and \emph{lost}, past both windows' upper
edges. Recall's window is nested inside recognition's at every rate. On a
twelve-per-octave grid $\beta_c / \theta = 0.16$--$0.18$ at ten cells
(Amendment 18). $\beta_r$ is $6$ to $40$ times weaker, and is not a fixed
fraction of $\theta$: $\beta_r \approx 0.34/k$ at $n = 4000$ (Amendment 23).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Both onsets are load-assisted]\label{prop:loadassist} \MEASURED
At its onset rate, recall in a dense area ($p = 1/2$) holds only from
$158$--$1098$ stored items on (Amendment 18); recognition, at its onset rate,
only from $5200$--$7700$ stored items on, in every cell. Above each onset
both edges of the window fall as $\beta$ rises: the strongest memory sits at
the weakest write that works, once enough has been stored.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:loadassist} is the least expected fact here and has no
counterpart in the Hopfield phase diagram, whose retrieval phase is entered
by \emph{lowering} the load. Here, at the onset, storing more items is what
makes an item retrievable. A candidate mechanism, not established: a weak
write leaves the read-out to the random connectome's own attractor, which is
nearer no stored item; accumulated potentiation from many items flattens that
landscape until each item's own increment decides the competition. It is the
first target for the soft ($\vartheta > 0$) ensemble below.


\subsection{Toward deriving the onset}

\begin{conjecture}[The onset is an extreme-value condition on $\ln n$]\label{conj:onset} \OPEN
$\beta_c / \theta$ is a constant ($\approx 0.17$) because both the write's
signal and the competitors' fluctuation scale as in $\theta$, with the
\emph{union bound over all $n$ neurons} setting the extreme-value scale.
\end{conjecture}

Two readings of the records constrain any derivation. (i) The naive
read-side argument --- a half-cue's members must out-drive the top $k$ of
$n - k$ Gaussian competitors with spread $\sqrt{kp(1-p)/2}$, whose threshold
sits $\sqrt{2\ln(n/k)}$ spreads up --- gives
$\ln(1+\beta_c) \propto \sqrt{(1-p)\ln(n/k)/(pk)}$, so $\beta_c/\theta \propto
\sqrt{\ln(n/k)/\ln n}$. Across Amendment 18's cells ($n/k$ from $25$ to $400$)
that factor varies by $37\%$; the measured $\beta_c/\theta$ varies by $6\%$
($0.16$--$0.18$). The data prefer $\ln n$: the union-bound scale of the
projection-convergence theorems, not the $k$-of-$n$ quantile of one read.
(ii) The first item's mean internal co-firing count during its write (a
measure of how long its assembly held together) rises smoothly through the
onset, from $2$--$3$ below it to $5$--$6$ well above, with no jump at the onset
in any cell. The onset is therefore not the convergence of a single item's
write. Together with Proposition~\ref{prop:loadassist} (the onset opens only
after many items are stored), the noise that sets the onset is plausibly the
crosstalk accumulated over many stored items, which would tie the onset to
the in-degree law of Amendment 21. Registered next: whether the onset is a
transition at all (Amendment 24: finite-size sharpening of the per-brain
onset, and critical slowing of the read-out).

\begin{conjecture}[The onset is a saddle-node of the overlap map]\label{conj:saddle} \OPEN
Let $F_\beta(m)$ be the overlap with a stored assembly after one frozen,
masked round from a $k$-neuron state holding a fraction $m$ of it, the rest
drawn from another stored item. Recall's onset is the saddle-node at which a
stable fixed point $m_s$ near $1$ and an unstable one $m_u$ are born,
$\max_m [F_\beta(m) - m] = 0$; half-cue completion turns on when $m_u$ falls to
$1/2$. A saddle-node predicts the critical slowing of
Amendment~24: passage through the bottleneck takes $\sim \pi/\sqrt{\beta - \beta_c}$
rounds.
\end{conjecture}

An exploratory probe (not registered; 5 brains, $(4000, 60, 0.5)$, 300 items,
every tenth item read) motivates it. From a cue of $mk$ members ALONE, $F$ is
concave with a single stable fixed point that rises smoothly with $\beta$
($\approx 0.5$ at $0.12\theta$, $\approx 0.85$ at $0.17\theta$) -- no transition,
and in disagreement with the eight-round read (from a half cue, $0.04$ at
$0.14\theta$). From a full $k$-neuron state, the rest random or another item's,
$F$ is SIGMOIDAL: $F(m) < m$ for every $m$ up to $0.18\theta$ (every read-out
decays), and by $0.20\theta$ a stable fixed point near $0.95$ coexists with an
unstable one at $m_u \approx 0.50$ --- the size of the half cue. The clean-cue
map misses the onset because the competitors that matter are the other stored
assemblies a partial state also excites, not the background. The probe's
item-averaged map places the bifurcation at $0.18$--$0.20\theta$, slightly
above the measured onset ($0.16$--$0.18\theta$); the eight-round read rises over
the same range as a sigmoid of the item mix, which suggests that items reach
their own bifurcations at different rates (recent items earlier) and the
onset is where the median item does. The derivation it asks for is a
mean-field $F_\beta(m)$ for a clipped multiplicative count matrix under
$k$-WTA, whose saddle-node gives $\beta_c/\theta$.
Amendment~24 then measured the onset converging at $0.163$--$0.170\theta$ for
$n = 2000$ to $16000$, already as sharp as its grid resolves at every size;
its settling instrument (exact winner set at period $\le 2$) could not test
the slowing, because $42$--$88\%$ of read-outs never settle exactly even
where they complete. The prediction stands for the overlap's relaxation.

\section{Attractor or sequence: the margin identity}

Amendments~25 and~26 of the memory registration found that one area and one
causal Hebbian rule store attractors or replayable sequences according to
whether the activity holds still while it is written, and that the
refraction-to-plasticity ratio $s/\beta$ is the switch when the write stays
online. This section derives where the switch must lie and records where it
was measured.

Write $\mathrm{raw}_j(t)$ for neuron $j$'s total input at round $t$ (from the
previous round's winners and the stimulus), $b_j(t)$ for its refraction bias,
and $\mathrm{net}_j = \mathrm{raw}_j - b_j$ for what $k$-WTA ranks. Every win
charges $b_j \mathrel{+}= s\,\mathrm{raw}_j$; every synapse whose pre fired the
round before and whose post fires now is multiplied by $1 + \beta$, below the
clip.

\begin{proposition}[The margin identity]\label{prop:margin} \PROVED
Let $j$ win rounds $t$ and $t+1$, and let a fraction $q_j$ of
$\mathrm{raw}_j(t)$ arrive through synapses that were potentiated at round $t$
(sources that won both rounds $t-1$ and $t$, stimulus included). Below the
clip,
\[
\mathrm{net}_j(t+1) - \mathrm{net}_j(t) = (\beta\, q_j - s)\,\mathrm{raw}_j(t)
\]
when the active sources repeat, while a neuron that has not won keeps its
net unchanged. In particular, for a converged assembly ($q_j = 1$ for every
member, fixed stimulus) members gain margin over every non-member when
$s < \beta$, hold it exactly at $s = \beta$, and lose it at $(s - \beta)\,
\mathrm{raw}_j$ per round when $s > \beta$: \emph{no assembly is a stable
fixed point of the online write when refraction outpaces plasticity}, and the
activity must move.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Below the clip a potentiated synapse's weight is multiplied by $1+\beta$, so
the potentiated part of $\mathrm{raw}_j$ grows by $\beta q_j \mathrm{raw}_j$
when the same sources fire again; the bias grows by $s\,\mathrm{raw}_j$. A
neuron that did not win has no potentiated input synapse and no bias charge
from that round. At $q_j = 1$ the identity reduces to the repository's
$\mathrm{net}_{t+1} - \mathrm{net}_t = (\beta - s)\,\mathrm{raw}_t$
(\texttt{REFRACTION-CANCELS-CONVERGENCE}).
\end{proof}

The proposition fixes an upper bound: an online write can store attractors
only for $s < \beta$. Formation sits below it, because the assembly is not
yet converged when it must start to gain margin: the members' $q_j$ during
formation is below one, and the condition is $s < \beta\, \bar q$ with
$\bar q$ the potentiated fraction the forming assembly actually has.

\paragraph{Measured (exploratory, 5 brains, not registered).} The
during-write consecutive overlap falls through one half at
$s^*/\beta = 0.81$ to $0.92$ in all nine conditions probed ($(n,k,p) =
(4000,60,0.5)$, $(2000,60,0.5)$, $(4000,120,0.5)$, each at $0.5$, $1$ and
$2\,\theta$; $T = 8$): items hold ($0.73$--$0.96$) below $s/\beta = 0.8$ and
move every round ($\le 0.21$) at $s/\beta \ge 0.95$. The boundary lies just
below $s = \beta$, as the proposition requires, and rises weakly with the
write. The simplest zero-parameter guess -- that $\bar q$ is the
unrefracted formation's early consecutive overlap -- fails: those overlaps
are $0.2$ to $0.6$ in the first rounds and their trend in $\beta$ is
opposite to that of $s^*$. Deriving $\bar q$, and with it $s^*/\beta$, needs
the formation dynamics, not one round of them.

\begin{conjecture}[A near-universal switch]\label{conj:switch} \OPEN
In the regime $kp \ge 3\ln n$ and below the clip, the online write's switch
from attractors to sequences lies at $s^*/\beta \in [0.8, 0.95]$,
independent of $n$, $k$ and $p$, and moves toward $1$ as the write strengthens.
\end{conjecture}

\section{The sequence memory's budget: a load law, a hazard, and a token code}\label{sec:budget}

Amendments~26--34 established that an area whose refraction outpaces its
plasticity stores sequences it replays by itself, and that a refraction which
\emph{recovers} (its bias decaying as $e^{-1/\tau}$ per writing round) lengthens
what one area holds several-fold. Amendments~35--43 ask the questions a
compiler for assembly programs must answer before it allocates an area: how
much one area holds, how it fails, how its knobs act, what recurring elements
and noise cost, and what links between areas cost. The answers below are a
\emph{cost model}; most of its terms are measured, two of its consequences are
proved from a model, and its constant is not yet derived.

\paragraph{Setting.} An area $(n,k,p)$ written at $\beta=\theta=\sqrt{(1-p)\ln
n/(pk)}$, refraction $s = \beta/2$ recovering over $\tau$ rounds, one writing
round per element. Replay is one frozen, masked round per step from a
uniformly random half of the first element; a step \emph{succeeds} if the
read-out overlaps the stored state by at least $0.3$; a sequence is
\emph{whole} if every step succeeds. Reliability is the fraction of brains (or
of sequences) whole; $\rho_q$ is the load at which it falls through $q$.

\paragraph{Vocabulary and evidence.} A \emph{brain} is one independently seeded
connectome, regenerated from a hash; a \emph{cell} is a choice of $(n,k,p)$;
a \emph{ladder} is a geometric grid of loads at which a fresh memory is written
and read. \emph{Frozen} rounds do not write; \emph{masked} rounds ignore the
refraction bias. The \emph{tiling deadline} is the failure of a refraction that
never recovers: a sequence uses up the area's neurons every $n/k$ elements and
replay breaks at those multiples (Amendment~28). ``Amendment $N$'' cites a
registration in \texttt{PREREG\_refraction\_memory.md}, whose bars were fixed
before its data; every judged cell is one no earlier run had touched, on twenty
new brains. ``Exploratory'' marks a probe that was not registered (four to ten
brains) and is reported to explain a registration, never to support a claim.
Two limits apply to every number below. The crossings $\rho_q$ are
log-interpolated between ladder points $2^{1/8}$ to $2^{1/12}$ apart and are
reported without intervals; a bootstrap over brains is owed. And the reliability
of one sequence is all or none per brain, so its curve is an estimate from
twenty Bernoulli trials per point.

\subsection{The load variable}

\begin{definition}[The independent-count model]\label{def:icm}
The $L$ stored states are independent uniform $k$-subsets; transition $u$
increments the count of every present synapse from $s_u$ to $s_{u+1}$ by one;
weights are $(1+\beta)^c$ below the clip, linearised to $1 + \beta c$; the
successor states are \emph{not} selected by the connectome (the structural
advantage a real write gives a successor --- it is chosen among the neurons
its predecessor already drives --- is left out); normalisation by in-degree is
a common factor and drops out of every ratio below.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Interference load]\label{lem:load}
\PROVED\ in the independent-count model, to first order in $k/n$.
From a state holding a fraction $o$ of $s_t$, the members of $s_{t+1}$
receive a learned excess whose ratio to the standard deviation of the base
input is $o\,\beta\sqrt{kp/(1-p)}$; every neuron receives, from the other
transitions, an excess whose variance relative to the base input's is
\[
  \rho_\beta \;=\; \frac{L\,k^2\beta^2}{n^2(1-p)},
  \qquad\text{which at } \beta = \theta \text{ is}\qquad
  \rho \;=\; \frac{L\,k\,\ln n}{n^2 p},
\]
and the signal ratio is then $o\sqrt{\ln n}$ at every $(n,k,p)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The base input of a neuron from $k$ active sources has variance $kp(1-p)$ (unit
weights). A member of $s_{t+1}$ has $okp$ active potentiated inputs on average,
each worth $\beta$: the excess is $\beta okp$, and $\beta okp/\sqrt{kp(1-p)} =
o\beta\sqrt{kp/(1-p)}$, which is $o\sqrt{\ln n}$ at $\beta=\theta$. Any neuron
$j$ gains $\beta$ from an active $i$ for each other transition with $i\in s_u$,
$j \in s_{u+1}$ and $(i,j)$ present; the active state meets $s_u$ in $k^2/n$
neurons on average and $j \in s_{u+1}$ with probability $k/n$, so the number of
such gains is Poisson with mean $Lk^3p/n^2$ and the excess has variance
$\beta^2 Lk^3p/n^2$. Divided by $kp(1-p)$ this is $\rho_\beta$; with $\beta^2 =
(1-p)\ln n/(pk)$ it is $Lk\ln n/(n^2p)$.
\end{proof}

The lemma fixes the variable, not the critical value. Two remarks place it.
First, the $\ln n$ is not intrinsic to the substrate: it enters through the
choice $\beta = \theta$, the convergence threshold, which scales the write
with $\sqrt{\ln n}$. Written with $\alpha = L/n$ and the coding level $f =
k/n$, the law at $\beta = \theta$ reads $\alpha_c f \ln n / p = \rho_c$: the form of
the classical capacity of sparse associative memories, $\alpha_c f\ln(1/f) =
O(1)$ (Willshaw et al.\ 1969; Tsodyks and Feigel'man 1988), with $\ln n$ in place
of $\ln(1/f)$ because the write, not the coding level, sets the signal here.
An $\ln(1/f)$ form was fitted after the fact to the cells below and failed at
new cells with small $n/k$ (exploratory), which is what this remark predicts.
Second, the near coincidence of $\rho_c \approx 0.12$--$0.14$ with the Hopfield
storage ratio $\alpha_c \approx 0.138$ (Amit, Gutfreund and Sompolinsky 1985) is
a coincidence of normalisations: the two variables count different things.
Third, a reduced model of one replay step that \emph{does} include the
successors' selection (\texttt{probes/2026-10-08/mf2.py}: write-time
selection, the learned transition, Poisson interference, normalisation,
$k$-WTA) reproduces one-step overlap maps to $0.02$--$0.03$, yet its saddle-node
loads are $0.23$--$0.34$ against the measured $0.12$--$0.17$ at nine cells
(\texttt{mf\_sn.log}): a factor $0.48 \pm 0.05$, correlated $0.65$ with the
measurements across cells, which removes only a fifth of their spread
(coefficient of variation $13.6\% \to 10.9\%$). The model gets the shape and
misses a factor of two.
Its assumptions fail in two measured ways. When the mean count per synapse pair, $L(k/n)^2 = \rho\,kp/\ln
n$, approaches one, the multiplicative weights $(1+\beta)^c$ leave the linear
regime: at $kp/\ln n = 24$ replay fails at its first step from $\rho = 0.07$
(exploratory). And the counts are not independent (Section~\ref{sec:dispersion}).

\subsection{The law and its domain}

\begin{proposition}[The load law]\label{prop:loadlaw} \MEASURED
For $kp \ge 3\ln n$ and $L(k/n)^2 \lesssim 0.5$, one sequence replays whole on
every brain up to a critical load and on none a factor $1.07$--$1.15$ above it.
Registered at $\rho_{50} = 0.142$ from a survey of seven cells and confirmed at
two cells never run: $0.141$ at $(6000,90,0.4)$ and $0.115$ at $(12000,80,0.5)$
(Amendment~37). Out of regime the cliff comes earlier ($0.086$, $0.089$).
At $n/k = 300$ and $\tau = 64$ it is $0.081$ at three cells, below the law's
band and below its safe rule (Amendment~38, recorded as failed).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[The recovery time is matched to reuse]\label{prop:tau} \MEASURED
A neuron is used about once every $n/k$ elements. On the same brains,
$\rho_{50}$ at $\tau = n/k$ against $\tau = n/k/2$ is $0.202$ vs $0.122$,
$0.152$ vs $0.123$, $0.120$ vs $0.122$ and $0.102$ vs $0.112$ at $n/k = 20$, $50$,
$100$, $200$ (gain $1.65$, $1.24$, $0.98$, $0.91$; Amendment~41); $\tau = n/k/2$
restores the safe rule at $n/k = 200$--$300$ (Amendment~39). Under $\tau = n/k/2$
the critical load is nearly constant, $0.122$ from $n/k = 20$ to $100$.
\end{proposition}

Both flanks of the $\tau$ dependence have a reading, neither yet a derivation.
Below $n/k/2$ the reuse of neurons clumps: the variance-to-mean ratio of
per-neuron use counts is $7$--$12$ at $\tau = n/k/4$ (rich get richer), $0.3$--$0.4$ at
$n/k/2$ and $0.04$--$0.08$ at $n/k$ (exploratory), and $\rho_{50}$ collapses to
$0.06$ at $n/k/4$. Above the optimum the never-recovering refraction's
\emph{tiling deadline} returns: where $\tau = n/k$ is the worse choice, its
brains fail at step $91$--$192 \approx n/k$, the deadline's signature, rather
than within a few steps of the cue as every other failure in Amendments~37--43.

\begin{proposition}[Any split]\label{prop:split} \MEASURED
At equal total load, $L/16$ sequences of $16$, $L/64$ of $64$ and one of $L$
elements all replay whole up to $\rho = 0.09$ ($\rho_{90} = 0.111$--$0.132$ in
every arm). Past it one long sequence fails all or none, while short ones fail
one by one, every brain losing the same share within binomial scatter;
$\rho_{50}$ of the short arms is $1.13$--$1.20$ times the long one's
(Amendment~40).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Recurrence and noise]\label{prop:reuse} \MEASURED
Inside the safe budget, sequences whose elements recur up to five times each
(words drawn from a vocabulary of $L/5$) and replay with $5\%$ of each step's
winners replaced at random cost nothing; at $10\%$ noise the loss grows from
$3$--$10\%$ at $\rho = 0.05$ to $34$--$63\%$ at $0.11$ (Amendment~42).
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The cliff as a large deviation}\label{sec:hazard}

Proposition~\ref{prop:split} says the two kinds of load fail differently.
A model that accounts for both:

\begin{definition}[Capture and hazard]
At load $\rho$ the half cue captures a sequence's first step with probability
$c(\rho)$, and every later step fails independently with probability $h(\rho)$:
\[
  \Prob(\text{whole} \mid \ell, \rho) \;=\; c(\rho)\,\bigl(1-h(\rho)\bigr)^{\ell-1}.
\]
Write $h = e^{-I(\rho)}$, with $I$ decreasing: an \emph{escape exponent}. Calling
it a large-deviation rate function would presume a large parameter in which $I$
grows linearly; none is established ($kp$, the number of afferents a state
sends a neuron, is the candidate, and predicts cliffs that sharpen with $kp$).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[What the model implies for one long sequence]\label{prop:ld}
\PROVED\ within the model, for $c \approx 1$ and $h \ll 1$ at the cliff, and (2)
for $I$ linear across the cliff's width. Let
$\rho_q(L)$ be the load at which a sequence of $L$ elements is whole with
probability $q$. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item $I\bigl(\rho_q(L)\bigr) = \ln L - \ln(-\ln q)$: the cliff moves with the
  \emph{logarithm} of what is stored, $d\rho_{50}/d\ln L = 1/I'(\rho_{50}) < 0$;
\item $\rho_{10} - \rho_{90} = \bigl[\ln\ln 10 - \ln\ln(10/9)\bigr]/|I'| \approx
  3.08/|I'|$, independent of $L$ to first order: the cliff's width is set by
  how steeply the hazard rises, its location by how long the sequence is.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Prob(\text{whole}) = (1-h)^{L-1} \approx \exp(-Le^{-I})$; setting it to $q$
gives (1); differencing (1) between $q=0.9$ and $q=0.1$ and linearising $I$
gives (2).
\end{proof}

Fitted after the fact to Amendment~40's record, $(c,h)$ from the $16$- and
$64$-element arms at each load located its single sequences' cliffs
($5000$--$13000$ elements) to one ladder step at both cells. The fitted hazard
rises about three decades between $\rho = 0.11$ and $0.13$, so $|I'| \approx
1/0.005$--$1/0.007$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:ld}(2) gives $\rho_{10}/\rho_{90}
\approx 1.13$--$1.20$ at $\rho \approx 0.12$ --- \emph{wider} than the measured
$1.07$--$1.15$. The tension is not resolved: a slope fitted from two lengths
may underestimate $|I'|$ where the hazard turns up, or the escape exponent may
not be linear across the cliff. The hazard measured directly over three
decades decides between them. Above $\rho \approx 0.13$ the hazard levels off near $10^{-2}$
while $c$ falls ($0.4$--$0.65$ at $0.14$): long sequences die of the in-sequence
hazard, short ones at the cue. Amendment~43 registers the model's prediction at
two new cells: $(c,h)$ from $16$- and $64$-element sequences must place the
$\rho_{50}$ of $256$-, $1024$- and whole-load sequences within $5\%$.

\paragraph{Amendment 43, judged (2026-10-08).} K1, K2, K4 pass; K3 fails.
$\rho_{50}$ falls strictly through $\ell = 16, 64, 256, 1024$ and one whole-load
sequence ($\approx 10^4$) at both cells, and linearly in $\ln\ell$ ($-0.0027$ and
$-0.0032$ per e-fold, residuals $\le 0.0018$; fitted after the run): part~(1) of
Proposition~\ref{prop:ld} holds over three decades. The constant-hazard model
does not: fitted on $16$ and $64$ it places $256$ and $1024$ within $5\%$ in
$\rho_{50}$ ($-1.9$ to $-4.7\%$) but a whole-load sequence $4.5\%$ and $9.8\%$ low,
and point by point it is far off ($0.16$ predicted against $0.48$ measured for
$256$ elements at $\rho = 0.132$; $0.00$ against $0.40$ for $1024$). The hazard
falls along a sequence: survivors of the first steps are safer. The candidate
is the cue's transient --- the overlap climbs from $0.5$ toward its stable value
over several steps, at risk throughout --- which would make $c$ a survival over
the first few steps rather than one. This also reads the width tension above:
a slope fitted where the transient dominates underestimates $|I'|$ for the
steady hazard. Two flaws of the run, recorded: rounding totals to multiples of
$1024$ left $9$ and $12$ distinct loads of the $17$ registered, and at one cell
three crossings share one ladder step.

\begin{conjecture}[A two-phase hazard]\label{conj:twophase} \OPEN
A sequence's first failure has hazard $h_0(\rho)$ over the first $m \approx 3$
steps after the cue and a steady $h_\infty(\rho) \ll h_0$ thereafter; the cliff
of one long sequence is where $L h_\infty(\rho) \approx 1$, and $\rho_{50}(\ell)$ is
linear in $\ln \ell$ with slope $1/|I_\infty'|$. Measuring each sequence's
first-failure step decides it.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Kramers escape at a saddle-node]\label{conj:kramers} \OPEN
Replay is a Markov chain on the overlap $o$ with one-step map $F_\rho$ and
fluctuation $\sigma^2$ per step (the variance of the overlap's one-step change
at the stable state); the cliff's $\rho_c$ is the saddle-node of $F_\rho$
(Conjecture~\ref{conj:saddle}), and below it
$I(\rho) \simeq A(\rho_c - \rho)^{3/2}/\sigma^2$ --- the exponent of escape over
the cubic barrier left near a saddle-node with Gaussian noise in continuous
time (Kramers 1940; H\"anggi, Talkner and Borkovec 1990), so for a discrete map
with non-Gaussian noise the $3/2$ is itself a prediction. Then the load law's
``constant'' is $\rho_c$ less a length-dependent offset,
$\rho_{50}(L) \approx \rho_c - \bigl[\sigma^2 (\ln L + 0.37)/A\bigr]^{2/3}$, and
part of what looked like a drift with $n/k$ is a drift with $\ln L$ (larger
areas cliff at longer sequences).
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{Dispersion: what the constant may be made of}\label{sec:dispersion}

A parameter-free reduced model of one replay step (write-time selection, the
learned transition, Poisson interference, normalisation, $k$-WTA) reproduces
the measured overlap maps to $0.02$--$0.03$ but places the critical load about
twice too high. In the engine the interference is Poisson at light load and
about twice Poisson near the cliff. Two sources are seen (exploratory): uneven
reuse of neurons at short $\tau$ (above), and \emph{pair-level capture} --- at
$n/k = 300$ a single synapse pair reaches $70$ counts where independent counts
would give a maximum near $10$: a neuron used about $130$ times is followed by
the same partner $70$ of them, because once its synapse saturates at $w_{\max}$
it alone outweighs the base input's fluctuation and the write re-traces it (a
P\'olya urn per pair).

Let $\kappa$ be the variance of the learned interference onto non-target neurons
(from a stored state, at $\rho = 0.10$) divided by its value under independent
Poisson counts of the same mean. The conjecture that every knob acts through
$\kappa$ --- $\rho_{50} \approx \rho^{\mathrm{Pois}}_c / \kappa$, so that $\rho_{50}\kappa$ is
constant --- was measured before it was registered, at twelve cells and
recovery times of Proposition~\ref{prop:tau} and its probes (four brains each,
exploratory), and it fails in that form. $\kappa$ does carry the collapse at short
$\tau$: $8.5$ at $\tau = n/k/4$ (where $\rho_{50} = 0.066$) against $1.0$ at
$\tau = n/k$ ($0.160$), and $2.6$ at $n/k = 300$, $\tau = 64$ ($0.081$) against
$1.25$ under the rule ($0.105$); about half of the excess is a non-Poisson count
distribution (pair capture), half correlation between pairs. But $\rho_{50}\kappa$
ranges from $0.12$ to $0.56$; under $\tau = n/k/2$, $\kappa \approx 1.2$ at every $n/k$
from $20$ to $300$ while $\rho_{50}$ drifts from $0.122$ to $0.105$; and at the
$\tau$ peak $\kappa$ moves from $1.31$ to $0.95$ while $\rho_{50}$ rises $1.66$-fold.
At $\tau = n/k$ the interference is slightly \emph{under}-dispersed: refraction
spaces the reuse.

\begin{conjecture}[The peak is on the signal side]\label{conj:disp} \OPEN
Beyond the collapse at short $\tau$, which dispersion explains, the recovery
time acts on the \emph{signal}: which neurons the write selects as a state's
successors. With $\tau \approx n/k$ the eligible pool is the neurons idle for
about one reuse interval, and selection among them gives the successor a
larger share of structural (pre-existing) input from its predecessor than the
learned transition alone provides; the residual drift with $n/k$ under the rule
is the $\ln L$ offset of Proposition~\ref{prop:ld}. Measuring a successor's
base input from its predecessor against $\tau$ decides the first part.
\end{conjecture}

\subsection{A token code: variance components}

\begin{definition}[Type and context components]
Code the $j$-th occurrence of word $w$ as $x_{wj} = \mu_w + \varepsilon_{wj}$,
with $\mathrm{Var}(\mu) = \sigma^2_{\mathrm{type}}$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\varepsilon) =
\sigma^2_{\mathrm{ctx}}$. For sparse binary codes at density $f = k/n \ll 1$ the
correlation of two codes is their overlap less $f$, over $1 - f$; the
\emph{intraclass correlation} $\mathrm{ICC} = \sigma^2_{\mathrm{type}}/
(\sigma^2_{\mathrm{type}} + \sigma^2_{\mathrm{ctx}})$ is the expected correlation of
two occurrences of one word.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Tokens with a type trace]\label{prop:icc} \MEASURED
Two occurrences of a word in different sequences overlap by $0.072$--$0.105$;
two different words by $0.000$--$0.002$ (Amendment~42, two cells): the area
codes each occurrence apart, with $\mathrm{ICC} \approx 0.1$ --- a token code
carrying a trace of the type $50$--$100$ times above chance.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[What the trace allows]\label{prop:sb} \PROVED\ within the
variance-components model, for (2) with Poisson-distributed shared counts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Types by averaging.} An area that sums $m$ occurrences of a word
  holds an estimate of $\mu_w$ with reliability
  $R_m = m\,\mathrm{ICC}/\bigl(1+(m-1)\mathrm{ICC}\bigr)$ (Spearman--Brown): at
  $\mathrm{ICC} = 0.1$, $R_{10} = 0.53$, $R_{40} = 0.82$, and $R = 0.9$ needs
  $m = 81$ (Spearman 1910; Brown 1910). The type is the shrunken mean of its
  tokens, in the sense of empirical Bayes.
\item \emph{Types from one pair.} Deciding whether two tokens are the same word
  from their shared count (mean $\mathrm{ICC}\,k$ against $k^2/n$) has
  $d' \approx \sqrt{\mathrm{ICC}\,k}$ when $k/n \ll \mathrm{ICC}$: about $3$ at
  $k = 100$. Larger assemblies make types more legible.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) is the reliability of a mean of $m$ exchangeable measures with
intraclass correlation $\mathrm{ICC}$. (2): the separation of the means is
$\mathrm{ICC}\,k - k^2/n \approx \mathrm{ICC}\,k$, and the standard deviation of a
Poisson count with that mean is $\sqrt{\mathrm{ICC}\,k}$.
\end{proof}

Two cautions bound the proposition. A binary code is not a sum of a type
vector and a context vector; the model is a statement about second moments
(neuron $i$ fires in an occurrence of $w$ with a probability $\pi_{wi}$, and the
ICC is the share of variance in those probabilities across words), and it says
nothing about how the shared neurons are organised. And the shared count of
two occurrences need not be Poisson: if the shared core is the stimulus's own
strongest targets, nearly the same neurons in every context, its variance is
smaller and $d'$ larger than (2) gives. Both are measurable from the stored
states.

Proposition~\ref{prop:sb} turns the trace into predictions a registration can
test: how many exposures a lexical area needs before a word is stable, and that
same-word detection improves as $\sqrt{k}$. Heavier recurrence (twenty uses per
word) costs up to $36\%$ and, unlike Proposition~\ref{prop:split}, fails
\emph{brain by brain} (brains from $0.00$ to $0.99$ whole), apparently by each
brain's draw of sequences.

\begin{conjecture}[Consistency, not recurrence, sets the cost of reuse]\label{conj:grammar} \OPEN
When a word recurs, its occurrences' transitions are partly carried by the
shared component. If its successors are consistent across contexts (a grammar)
the shared transitions agree and heavy recurrence costs little and generalises
to unseen combinations; if they are drawn at random they conflict, and the cost
grows with recurrence. Heavy reuse under bigram statistics against shuffled
controls with the same word frequencies decides it.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Probed the same day: the conjecture reversed (exploratory).}
(\texttt{probes/2026-10-08/probe\_grammar*.py}; $(4000,100,0.35)$, $\tau = 40$,
twenty uses per word, ten brains, each its own grammar.) Every word of a
vocabulary of $V$ was given $b$ allowed successors and the sequences were random
walks on that grammar. Scored by \emph{word} --- the read-out's nearest stored
token, any sequence, and that token's word --- replay is whole for $0.98$ of
sequences under i.i.d.\ words ($b = V$), but $0.19$--$0.39$ at $b = 1$, $0.35$ and
$0.00$ at $b = 4$, and $0.00$ at $b = 2$ (two loads). Dropping the $0.3$ match
threshold changes nothing: the read-out lands on wrong \emph{words}, not on
blends. Consistency made reuse costly, not cheap, and the same-word overlap
rose with it (to $0.42$ at $b = 1$): repeated transitions pull a word's tokens
toward a shared code and merge their contexts. What varies with $b$ at fixed
word frequency is how often each \emph{transition} is stored ($\approx U/b$:
about $1$, $5$, $10$, $20$ repeats per bigram).

\begin{conjecture}[Transition repetition sets the cost of reuse]\label{conj:bigram} \OPEN
A token store pays for recurrence through the repetition of transitions, not
of elements: at a fixed number of uses per word, replay fails once the same
bigram is stored more than a few times, and at a fixed number of repeats per
bigram the number of uses per word does not matter. If so, a corpus with
frequent bigrams cannot be held in one such area: its statistics must be
consolidated in a separate, type-level store --- the complementary-learning-systems
division (McClelland, McNaughton and O'Reilly 1995), here forced by a failure rather than assumed.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Amendment 44, judged (2026-10-08).} G1 and G4 pass, G3 passes
vacuously, G2 fails. About five repetitions per transition destroy word-level
replay on every brain at both cells whatever the uses per word ($10$, $20$,
$40$), while random successors at twenty uses keep $0.77$ and $0.95$; repeated
transitions raise the same-word overlap by $0.05$--$0.06$. The first half of
Conjecture~\ref{conj:bigram} holds with a vengeance; its second half (uses do
not matter at fixed repetition) is untested, because every repeated arm sits
on the floor, where the bar written for a graded response cannot
discriminate --- a design lesson: a bar on a spread needs arms off the floor.
Recurrence alone is not free: at twenty uses with random successors, whole
brains collapse ($1$ and $4$ of $20$), and the collapsed brains are not those
whose draws repeat bigrams most (correlations $0.01$--$0.16$, recomputed from
the seeds after the run). The threshold between one and five repetitions, and
the collapse, are the open measurements.

\paragraph{The reuse budget has two edges (exploratory, after Amendment 44).}
(\texttt{probe\_dose2.py}, \texttt{probe\_product.py},
\texttt{probe\_edges.py}; $(12000,80,0.45)$, $\tau = 75$, $\rho = 0.05$, ten
brains.) Word-level reliability falls through two sharp edges. Recurrence,
with random successors: $1.00$, $1.00$, $0.39$, $0.00$, $0.00$ at $U = 10$, $20$,
$40$, $60$, $80$ uses per word, failing brain by brain (brains $0.00$--$0.97$ at
$U = 40$), with the same-word overlap \emph{falling} as $U$ grows ($0.071 \to
0.036$). Repetition, at $U = 10$: $1.00$, $0.95$, $0.39$, $0.00$, $0.00$ at $R = 1.0$,
$2.4$, $2.8$, $3.7$, $5.7$ repeats per transition. Neither $R$, nor $U$, nor $UR$
orders the interior points (at $UR \approx 55$--$68$: $0.00$, $0.39$, $0.28$), but
the product of the two marginal curves does: $f(R)\,g(U)$ predicts $(U,b) =
(20,8)$, $(12,4)$, $(24,16)$, $(40,32)$, $(48,64)$ at $0.45$, $0.15$, $0.92$,
$0.37$, $\approx0.2$ against $0.39$, $0.18$, $0.89$, $0.28$, $0.21$. A smaller cell,
$(4000,100,0.35)$ at $\tau = n/k$, tolerates more of both (random reuse at
$R \approx 5$ keeps $0.91$; \texttt{probe\_dose.py}): the edges move with the cell.

\begin{conjecture}[A separable reuse budget]\label{conj:separable} \OPEN
A token store's word-level reliability under reuse is the product of a
repetition term and a recurrence term, $P(U,R) \approx f(R)\,g(U)$, with $f$
falling through an edge $R^*$ in repeats per transition (graded across brains)
and $g$ through an edge $U^*$ in uses per element (whole brains collapsing);
the edges move with the cell, the product form does not.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{Amendment 45, judged (2026-10-08).} S1 passes, S2 fails narrowly.
Both edges stand at two new cells, where the probe put them: recurrence near
$40$ uses ($0.48$, $0.51$), repetition near $2.8$ repeats ($0.35$, $0.68$) --- across
three cells with $n/k = 117$--$150$ the edges hardly move. The product law was
tested informatively (four of five predictions off the floor and ceiling per
cell) and predicts the interior to a mean error of $0.05$--$0.08$, but one arm of
ten misses the $0.15$ tolerance by $0.006$. With brains spread from $0$ to $1$ in
most interior arms, an arm's mean has a standard error of $0.07$--$0.09$, so the
bar was tight; it is recorded as failed. Conjecture~\ref{conj:separable} stands
as approximately right and unestablished. Two mechanistic tests come next: the
repetition edge should scale as $R^* \propto 1/\ln(1+\beta)$ if multiplicative
core-to-core links carry it, and in collapsed brains failed replays should
converge on a few shared states if a spurious hub attractor carries the
recurrence edge.

\paragraph{The collapse is a spurious attractor, and replay-time adaptation
controls it (exploratory).} (\texttt{probe\_collapse.py},
\texttt{probe\_control.py}; $(10000,75,0.48)$, forty uses per word, random
successors, twenty brains.) In a collapsed brain the failed replays funnel into
a handful of shared states --- $7$ to $36$ distinct end states among about $200$
failures, mean pairwise overlap $0.11$--$0.19$ against $0.0075$ by chance ---
while in a partly failing brain every failure ends somewhere new (overlap
$0.01$--$0.05$). The attractor is not made of hubs: its neurons are used about
$1.1$ times as often as average and barely overlap the brain's most-used
neurons ($0.00$--$0.20$). It is causal: suppressing the $2k$ neurons the failed
replays of one half of the sequences converge on, while replaying the other
half, raises partly failing brains from $0.17$, $0.30$, $0.11$ to $0.82$, $0.79$,
$0.94$ (the deepest collapses do not recover from so small a lesion). And it is
controllable by the force replay lacks: replay runs masked, with no adaptation
at all, and a mild session adaptation --- each replay winner charged $0.1$ of
its drive, decaying over $50$ steps, kept across sequences --- lifts every
collapsed brain (from $0.04$ to $0.46$ on average, each to $0.31$--$0.51$) and
the intact brains too ($0.74 \to 0.88$); a stronger one ($0.3$) suppresses
correct states as well and replay fails everywhere. The anti-capture force has
a window at read time as it has at write time.

\begin{conjecture}[Capture is controlled by adaptation at both ends]\label{conj:readcontrol} \OPEN
Every failure of this memory is a capture --- of neurons, synapse pairs, token
cores or the whole area --- and adaptation is the force that opposes it: at
write time (refraction, $\tau$ matched to reuse) and at read time (a session
adaptation charging replay winners). A store with both, each inside its
window, holds reuse that one without read-time adaptation cannot.
\end{conjecture}

\paragraph{The survival curve along a sequence (exploratory).}
(\texttt{probe\_survival.py}; $(8000,80,0.5)$, $\tau = 50$, $256$-element
sequences.) No sequence fails in its first five steps at any load; the
per-step hazard then \emph{falls} along the sequence --- at $\rho = 0.14$:
$2.2\times10^{-2}$ at steps $6$--$10$, $1.1\times10^{-2}$ at $11$--$30$,
$2.7\times10^{-3}$ at $31$--$100$, $4.7\times10^{-4}$ at $101$--$255$, roughly
$h(j) \propto j^{-1.2\ \text{to}\ -1.7}$ --- and the overlap of surviving read-outs
climbs more slowly the higher the load ($0.75 \to 0.92$ over eight steps at
$\rho = 0.12$, $0.73 \to 0.80$ at $0.14$). Conjecture~\ref{conj:twophase}'s
three-step transient is wrong: the transient is long and lengthens with load,
as critical slowing near the saddle-node would make it, and a hazard decaying
as $1/j$ gives power-law survival, which is one way to obtain the linear law in
$\ln\ell$.

\paragraph{The signal side of the $\tau$ peak (exploratory).}
(\texttt{probe\_signal.py}, the twelve cases of the dispersion probe.) A
successor's structural input from its predecessor is $z = 1.3$--$2.0$ standard
deviations above a random neuron's --- comparable to the learned signal
($\sqrt{\ln n} \approx 2.8$--$3.2$ at full overlap), so the omission in
Definition~\ref{def:icm} is a large one --- but it is slightly \emph{lower} at
$\tau = n/k$ than at $n/k/2$ ($1.32$ against $1.39$ at $n/k = 20$, where $\rho_{50}$
rises $1.66$-fold). Conjecture~\ref{conj:disp} fails as stated: neither the
variance of interference nor the selection of successors carries the peak. The
next candidate is the \emph{tail} of the interference at the $k$-WTA threshold,
which neither statistic sees.

\paragraph{Where the variance sits decides the sample size.} In
Proposition~\ref{prop:split} the brain-level intraclass correlation is near zero
(each brain's whole fraction within binomial scatter), so sequences are
independent trials and the effective sample is brains times sequences; under
heavy reuse it is high, the effective sample is twenty brains, and inference
must be hierarchical (a random intercept per brain).

\subsection{Links between areas}

Amendment~33 linked each plan position's state in a chunk area $C$ to its
chunk's first state in a sequence area $S$ through a fiber written twice per
association. An exploratory sweep of $A$ associations onto $J$ chunk starts
(fan-in $F = A/J$; $k = 60$, $p = 1/2$, ten brains) at four area pairs finds
that the analogue of Lemma~\ref{lem:load}, $\rho_x = Ak\ln n_S/(n_Cn_Sp)$, is
\emph{not} the variable. The fraction of starts evoked declines gradually,
without a cliff, and the number of associations at which half are evoked,
$A_{50}$, depends on the \emph{source} area alone:

\toprule
$(n_S, n_C)$ & $A_{50}$, $J = 16$ & $A_{50}$, $J = 64$ \\
\midrule
$(4000, 2000)$ & $1810$ & $2400$ \\
$(8000, 2000)$ & $1810$ & $2365$ \\
$(4000, 4000)$ & $2455$ & $> 3860$ \\
$(8000, 4000)$ & $2455$ & $4115$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent (log-interpolated; at $(4000,4000)$, $J = 64$ the sweep ended at
$0.525$.) Doubling the target area changes $A_{50}$ by less than $2\%$; doubling
the source area multiplies it by $1.36$ ($J = 16$) and about $1.7$ ($J = 64$), an
exponent of $0.45$--$0.8$ in $n_C$ that grows with the number of targets
(two sizes of each area, one $k$, ten brains: the exponent is a slope between
two points, and the dependence on $k$ is untested). The
competition is among whole target assemblies, and what decides it is how much
the cue resembles the other source states linked to each wrong target --- a
quantity of the source area ($\sim k/n_C$ per state, summed over the fan-in),
not of the target's $n_S$ neurons. The wrong start that wins is the one with
the most accumulated links: a hub.

\begin{conjecture}[The link budget is a source-area law]\label{conj:links} \OPEN
The associations a fiber $C \to S$ carries onto $J$ target assemblies at a
given fidelity scale with the source area as $n_C/k$ up to an extreme-value
factor in $J$ (the most-linked wrong target), and not with $n_S$. A hierarchy's
depth is then bounded by its chunk areas' sizes, not its sequence areas'.
\end{conjecture}

\noindent The link budget is the first a hierarchy exhausts (Amendment~33).

\section{Ensembles and entropy}

The configuration entropy of one area is
\(
  S \;=\; \ln\binom{n}{k} \;\approx\; k\ln(n/k) + k
\)
nats, an upper bound on what an area can represent. Capacity questions then
become counting arguments: the $M$ stored assemblies must be mutually
distinguishable under the read-out, and $\alpha$ measures how much of the
configuration space is committed.

A canonical ensemble can be introduced by softening $\wta$: assign to each
state $x$ the energy $E(x) = -\sum_{i \in x} I_i$ and the Gibbs measure
$\Prob(x) \propto e^{-E(x)/\vartheta}$ over $k$-subsets, with partition function
$Z(\vartheta) = \sum_{|x|=k} e^{-E(x)/\vartheta}$. Deterministic $k$-WTA is the
$\vartheta \to 0$ limit. This is the natural route to a free energy for the
substrate, and it is worth noting that $\vartheta$ is not currently a parameter of
the implementation --- introducing it would be a modelling choice, not a
measurement, and should be kept clearly separate from the results above.
